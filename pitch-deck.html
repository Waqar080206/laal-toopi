RAKSHAM Round 1 · Track 03 · Deepfake Detection for Financial Communications

TRUVY

Know what's real.

One-sentence solution: TRUVY is a verification gate in front of payment release — it weighs audio and video forensics, provenance, language, pressure-language and payment-context signals, never acts on one weak signal, and routes every doubtful instruction through an out-of-band callback and a human reviewer, leaving an evidence record for the fraud team.

The Problem

Who gets hurt, and in which minute

A social hack wrapped around a technical fake.

02
The scene (illustrative, not a real case)

6:42 pm on a Friday. Meera, a payments approver at a mid-sized lender in Delhi, gets a WhatsApp voice note — the managing director, in his familiar Hindi-English cadence, asking her to release a transfer of ₹[amount] to a vendor’s new account before cut-off. He is boarding a flight and cannot take a call. The voice was cloned from public interview clips, and a short deepfake video of him nodding approval follows to seal the request.

Maker-checker controls exist — but the pressure is social, not technical, and the person holding the key is the one being pressured.
Primary user

The payments or treasury approver at a bank, NBFC or company.

Harmful moment

The minute between hearing a trusted voice and releasing funds.

Exact threat pattern

A cloned executive voice or deepfaked video requesting an urgent transfer or beneficiary change — while discouraging verification.

Why India

UPI, digital banking and wallets move money on fast rails — and fraudsters now run on AI.

Second difficulty

Many languages, heavy code-switching, accents detectors handle poorly.

Bounded scope

Voice notes and video that instruct or approve payments. Documents & live calls are named extensions.

Core Decision-Making

Many signals, one safe next step

The Gate is useful when right and harmless when wrong.

04
Signals & weight rule
  • Forensic scores — audio/video signs of synthesis; audio down-weighted on low-confidence languages.
  • Language identification — sets the detector’s confidence ceiling.
  • Provenance (C2PA) — presence helps; absence is neutral.
  • Pressure cues — urgency, secrecy, “don’t call back”, authority claims; structured flags only.
  • Payment context — amount vs history, new beneficiary, off-hours, channel. Strongest signal.
Risk bands
Low
Routine, context normal → proceed via maker-checker, log the case.
Elevated
Context/pressure flags or inconclusive detector → mandatory callback.
High
Multiple independent signals agree → hold, human reviewer, dual approval.
Rules the Gate obeys
  1. Never act on a single weak signal.
  2. New/changed beneficiary above a set amount + any pressure cue → callback, regardless of audio score.
  3. Labels: “Consistent with genuine media”, “Inconclusive”, “Signs of synthesis”. No first label in low-confidence languages.
  4. Missing provenance is neutral, never incriminating.
  5. Payments are held, never auto-rejected.
  6. Reviewer may override with a written, logged reason.
  7. Callback numbers only from the institution’s directory.
What the approver sees

“Hold recommended. This request asks for an urgent payment to a new beneficiary outside business hours, and the audio shows signs of synthetic speech. Detector confidence is reduced because the clip is in Hindi. Next step: call the managing director on the number in the company directory — not the one in the message.”

Responsible AI

Privacy, safety & misuse safeguards

06
The brief asksOur answer
What data do you need?The instruction, its metadata, and payment context. We do not collect customer personal data, credentials or voiceprints — no biometric database. Prototype uses only synthetic, public or consented data.
What happens when wrong?No automatic blocking, no accusation from one weak signal, reviewer override, tuning from outcomes.
Who can act on it?Approver may request a callback or hold. Only a human reviewer clears a High hold, with dual approval.
How will users understand it?Plain-language reasons and a named next step — a score alone is never the explanation.
How does it resist misuse?Requester never sees the score. Rate-limited submissions. Role-based, logged access. Any LLM step treats transcripts as untrusted text and may emit structured cue flags only — never a decision.
Further safeguards
  • Raw audio/video processed ephemerally, deleted after scoring unless escalated — then retained encrypted, limited access, fixed retention.
  • Stored records: hashes, scores, reasons, timestamps — in a tamper-evident log.
  • Bias named openly: accuracy varies by language/accent → language-aware ceiling + reviewer escalation as mitigation.
When wrong

False positive — a genuine executive flagged: cost is a short callback delay; shadow mode measures this.

False negative — a clone fools the detector: Rule 2 still fires on payment context → callback gate anyway.

Security posture

Inside the institution’s network boundary, so audio never leaves it. Encryption in transit & at rest, RBAC, tamper-evident audit log, rate limiting. Decision kept partly independent of the detector for adversarial adaptation.

Stateless & extensible

Impact

Why TRUVY wins

Defence in depth: useful when right, harmless when wrong.

10
1 · It still works when the detector fails.

Payment context and a callback do not depend on audio quality.

2 · It is honest about Indian languages.

Shows low confidence instead of false certainty — and tests the language gap rather than assuming it.

3 · It ends in action.

Every verdict leads to a callback, a review or a report pack — never just a score.

4 · It is buildable.

Narrow scope, a three-scenario demo, hourly checkpoints and a fallback if anything breaks.

Where this lands for an Indian BFSI institution

A gate in front of payment release that turns a cloned voice or deepfaked video into a held payment, a verified callback and a report pack — instead of a wire that leaves the building. Detection is swappable; the decision logic, verification path and evidence trail are the product.
